Require an explicit etymology before two words share a family

nuk, the negator, was filed under not, the noun meaning swimming, because
its etymology compares the word to English and the parents extractor took
that English gloss as an Albanian ancestor. build_families also trusted the
derived field, which is a co-occurrence list: bri, rib, listed bori, an
Ottoman loan for bugle, and zbres, descend, listed falas, falem and fale.

A word now joins a family only when its etymology text states a
composition, which Wiktionary writes as base plus affix. Requiring the plus
sign is what separates a derivation from a mention, so a cognate such as
tmerr, terror, gets no edge. Spelling similarity is deliberately not used:
marre, shame, and marrte, twilight, are homographs of marr with unrelated
meanings.

Effects: 3,082 families, was 3,278. 381 multi-member, was 245. 1,045 levels.
marr is now exactly marr, marres and merrem. nuk is its own root in level 2;
not is a separate root at level 301. per is a genuine 56-member family over
7 levels, since the prefix really does have that many attested derivatives.

New tools/test_derivations.py pins the rule in both directions, 25 cases,
including the real defects and four derivations that must still be found.

validate.py gains a family-wide part-consistency check, fixing two bugs
there: a continuation was compared against part 1 instead of the previous
part, and each level was checked in isolation so part 2 of 10 beside part 1
of 7 passed.

Verified: data validation, 177 tests, POS regression, Anki export read back
the way Anki reads it, 1,045 decks and 3,731 notes, and 16 of 16 in Firefox
with zero console errors.
